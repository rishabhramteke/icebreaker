# Writing Icebreaker lines

The deck is for practising saying hello to strangers of any gender, in any setting, not only romantic ones. A line has to be something a stranger can hear comfortably from someone they have never met.

## Rules

1. **Notice a choice, an action or the shared moment.** Comment on what the person picked (a book, a jacket, a coffee order, a dog's name), did (helped someone, asked a good question), or on the place you are both in (the queue, the view, the menu). Never comment on their body, and do not guess their character from their looks ("you have kind eyes", "you look like a strong woman").
2. **No labels about identity.** Nothing about gender, age, nationality or looks ("badass woman", "strong independent lady").
3. **Plain beats clever.** Direct or ordinary openers are rated better than cute or flippant lines (Kleinke, Meeker and Staneski 1986). No pick-up-artist routines, no backhanded compliments, no cold-reading tricks.
4. **Say what is true about you when it helps.** Honest openers ("I'm new here", "I'm practising talking to people") lower the stakes for both sides.
5. **End on a real question** the other person can answer easily, so the conversation has somewhere to go.
6. **Leave an easy way out.** Lines must not pressure or corner anyone. Respect signals such as headphones, hurry or a closed posture; the app's dare is to say hello, not to keep talking.
7. **Why it is worth doing:** people underestimate how much strangers enjoy talking with them (Boothby et al. 2018, the "liking gap"; Epley and Schroeder 2014) and how good a compliment makes someone feel (Zhao and Epley 2021). The lines should make starting easy, not impressive.

## Checks before committing a line

- Would it feel fine said by anyone to anyone, at a bus stop, in daylight?
- Is the compliment about a choice or action, not the body or a guessed trait?
- Does it end with something easy to answer, or a simple hello?
